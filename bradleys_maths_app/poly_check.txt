Q: The cubic polynomial $h(x)$ is defined by $h(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$.<br><br>1. Show that $(x + 1)$ is a factor of $h(x)$, and express $h(x)$ as a product of linear factors (one of which is repeated).<br><br>2. Sketch the graph of $y = h(x)$, indicating the coordinates of any points of intersection with the axes.<br><br>3. Solve the inequality $h(x) > 0$, giving your answer in set notation.<br><br>4. The graph of $y = h(x)$ is transformed by a stretch parallel to the $y$-axis with scale factor $3$. Find the equation of the transformed graph.
S1: To prove $(x + 1)$ is a factor, evaluate $h(-1)$ using the factor theorem:<br>\begin{aligned}h(-1) &= (-1)^3 - 3(-1)^2 + 4 \cr &= -1 - 3 + 4 \cr &= 0\end{aligned}<br><br>Since $h(-1) = 0$, $(x + 1)$ is indeed a factor of $h(x)$.
S2: Divide $x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$ by $(x + 1)$ to find the quadratic factor:<br>$$h(x) = (x + 1)(x^2 - 4x + 4)$$
S3: Factorise the quadratic factor $x^2 - 4x + 4$:<br>\begin{aligned}x^2 - 4x + 4 &= (x - 2)^2\end{aligned}<br><br>Thus, the fully factorised form with a repeated factor is:<br>\begin{aligned}h(x) &= (x + 1)(x - 2)^2\end{aligned}
S4: To sketch the graph of $y = h(x)$, determine the intercepts:<br><br>The $x$-intercepts occur where $h(x) = 0 \implies x = -1$ and $x = 2$ (repeated root). The points are $(-1, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$.<br><br>The $y$-intercept occurs where $x = 0 \implies h(0) = 4$. This is the point $(0, 4)$.<br><br>Sketch the curve. Note that because $x = 2$ is a repeated root of even multiplicity, the curve touches the $x$-axis and turns around at $(2, 0)$ instead of crossing it:<br><br><img src='images/Pure_PNGs/004173_ans.png' style='width:100%; max-width:400px; margin: 15px auto; display:block; border: 1px solid #ccc;'></img>
S5: To solve the inequality $h(x) > 0$, find where the curve lies strictly above the $x$-axis. From our sketch, the curve is strictly positive for $x > -1$, except at the repeated root $x = 2$ where $h(2) = 0$.<br><br>Write this solution in set notation:<br>$$ {x : -1 < x < 2 } \cup {x : x > 2 }$$
S6: A stretch parallel to the $y$-axis with a scale factor of $3$ multiplies the entire function by $3$:<br>\begin{aligned}y &= 3h(x) \cr &= 3(x^3 - 3x^2 + 4) \cr &= 3x^3 - 9x^2 + 12\end{aligned}
S7: Final Answer:<br>\begin{aligned}&(x + 1)(x - 2)^2 \cr & {x : -1 < x < 2 } \cup {x : x > 2 } \cr &y = 3x^3 - 9x^2 + 12\end{aligned}
INSIGHT [The Head Teacher's Eye: Repeated Roots and Inequalities]: Pay extremely close attention to the inequality sign when dealing with repeated roots that touch the axis. If the inequality is strict ($> 0$), you must exclude the touching point from your solution set. If it is non-strict ($\ge 0$), the touching point is included.

Q: The cubic polynomial $f(x)$ is defined by $f(x) = 3x^2 - x^3 - 4$.<br><br>1. Show that $x = -1$ is a root of $f(x) = 0$, and express $f(x)$ in a fully factorised form.<br><br>2. Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, indicating the coordinates of any points of intersection with the axes.<br><br>3. Solve the inequality $f(x) \ge 0$, giving your answer in set notation.<br><br>4. The graph of $y = f(x)$ is transformed by a reflection in the $x$-axis. Find the equation of the transformed graph.
S1: To prove $x = -1$ is a root, substitute $-1$ into the polynomial $f(x)$:<br>\begin{aligned}f(-1) &= 3(-1)^2 - (-1)^3 - 4 \cr &= 3(1) - (-1) - 4 \cr &= 3 + 1 - 4 \cr &= 0\end{aligned}<br><br>Since $f(-1) = 0$, $x = -1$ is indeed a root, and $(x + 1)$ is a factor of $f(x)$.
S2: Divide $3x^2 - x^3 - 4$ (rearranged as $-x^3 + 3x^2 - 4$) by $(x + 1)$ to find the quadratic factor:<br>$$f(x) = (x + 1)(-x^2 + 4x - 4)$$
S3: Factorise out the negative sign to simplify:<br>\begin{aligned}f(x) &= -(x + 1)(x^2 - 4x + 4) \cr &= -(x + 1)(x - 2)^2\end{aligned}<br><br>This is the fully factorised form.
S4: To sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, determine the axes intercepts:<br><br>The $x$-intercepts occur where $f(x) = 0 \implies x = -1$ and $x = 2$ (repeated root). This gives the points $(-1, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$.<br><br>The $y$-intercept occurs where $x = 0 \implies f(0) = -4$. This is the point $(0, -4)$.<br><br>Since the leading coefficient is negative ($-1$), the curve starts in the top-left and heads down to the bottom-right. It touches the axis and turns around at $(2, 0)$:<br><br><img src='images/Pure_PNGs/004174_ans.png' style='width:100%; max-width:400px; margin: 15px auto; display:block; border: 1px solid #ccc;'></img>
S5: To solve the inequality $f(x) \ge 0$, find where the curve lies on or above the $x$-axis. From our sketch, this occurs for $x \le -1$. The curve also touches the axis at $x = 2$, which satisfies $f(2) = 0 \ge 0$.<br><br>Write this combined solution in set notation:<br>$$ {x : x \le -1 } \cup {2 }$$
S6: A reflection in the $x$-axis replaces $y$ with $-y$:<br>\begin{aligned}-y &= f(x) \cr y &= -f(x)\end{aligned}<br><br>\begin{aligned}y &= -(3x^2 - x^3 - 4) \cr &= x^3 - 3x^2 + 4\end{aligned}
S7: Final Answer:<br>\begin{aligned}&-(x + 1)(x - 2)^2 \cr & {x : x \le -1 } \cup {2 } \cr &y = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4\end{aligned}
INSIGHT [The Head Teacher's Eye: Disjoint Single Points]: When solving non-strict inequalities involving negative cubics with repeated roots, keep an eye out for disjoint single-point solutions. The point where the curve touches and turns around from the axis is a single isolated solution that must be represented in set notation using curly brackets: $ {a }$.

Q: In this question you must show detailed reasoning.<br><br>The cubic polynomial $f(x)$ is given by:\begin{aligned} f(x) = 2x^3 - x^2 - 13x - 6 \end{aligned}1. Use the Factor Theorem to show that $(x - 3)$ is a linear factor of $f(x)$.<br><br>2. Hence, express $f(x)$ as the product of three linear factors.
S1: To prove that $(x - 3)$ is a factor of $f(x) = 2x^3 - x^2 - 13x - 6$, apply the Factor Theorem and evaluate $f(3)$:\begin{aligned} f(3) &= 2(3)^3 - (3)^2 - 13(3) - 6 \cr &= 2(27) - 9 - 39 - 6 \cr &= 54 - 9 - 39 - 6 \cr &= 0 \end{aligned}Since $f(3) = 0$, by the Factor Theorem, $(x - 3)$ is a linear factor of $f(x)$.
S2: Perform polynomial division or equate coefficients to divide $f(x)$ by $(x - 3)$ and find the quadratic quotient:\begin{aligned} &2x^3 - x^2 - 13x - 6 \cr &\quad = (x - 3)(2x^2 + ax + 2) \end{aligned}Compare the $x^2$ coefficient on both sides:\begin{aligned} -1 &= -3(2) + a \cr -1 &= -6 + a \cr a &= 5 \end{aligned}So, the quadratic quotient is $2x^2 + 5x + 2$.
S3: Fully factorise the quadratic quotient $2x^2 + 5x + 2$ into two linear factors:\begin{aligned} 2x^2 + 5x + 2 = (2x + 1)(x + 2) \end{aligned}Combine all three factors to express $f(x)$ in its fully factorised form:\begin{aligned} &f(x) \cr &\quad = (x - 3)(2x + 1)(x + 2) \end{aligned}
S4: Final Answer: \begin{aligned} &f(x) \cr &\quad = (x - 3)(2x + 1)(x + 2) \end{aligned}
INSIGHT [The Head Teacher's Eye: Equating Coefficients over Long Division]: For factorising cubic polynomials once a root is known, equating coefficients is much faster than running a full synthetic or polynomial division. Since $2x^3$ requires the lead quadratic term to be $2x^2$, and $-6$ requires the constant term to be $+2$ when divided by $-3$, you only need a single comparison to find the middle coefficient $a$.
